# Type: profile

A description of an entity the assistant needs to understand: a company, brand, product, service, audience, persona, project, or a person (the user, a key client contact). It is consulted to ground any work about that entity, so the model doesn't fall back on generic assumptions.

## What makes it usable
- What the entity is, in one or two plain sentences, and what it is often confused with.
- The facts that change the work: what it offers, to whom, how it differs, where it operates, its size or stage, dated when they change.
- For a person: role, responsibilities, what they decide, and how they like to work and be addressed.
- For an audience or persona: who they are, what they need and already know, the words they use, what puts them off.

## Diagnosis questions
- Which tasks need this description, and what goes wrong today without it?
- In one sentence, what is it and who is it for? What would a newcomer get wrong?
- Which facts are stable, and which change every quarter?
- For an audience: is there research, data or real quotes, or is it the team's view?
- Materials that help: an about page, a pitch deck, a positioning document, product sheets, audience research, real customer quotes.

## Typical skeleton
1. In short: what it is, for whom
2. Offer
3. Audience
4. Differences from alternatives, in concrete terms
5. Facts and figures, dated
6. What it is not, and common confusions

## Rules for this type
- Write in the third person, by name, not as "we": the assistant reads the file about someone else.
- Turn claims into observable facts: not "customer-focused" but "answers support requests within two hours, seven days a week". Marketing copy pasted as description gives the model adjectives and no facts.
- Label aspirational positioning as positioning, not as a fact about the market.
- For an audience, mark what comes from research and what is the team's hypothesis.
- Give each entity its own heading when the file covers several.
- Leave voice and tone rules to a guidelines file.

## Sample entry for the plan
The "In short" section as it will appear, plus one dated fact.
