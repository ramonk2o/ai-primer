# Type: guidelines

Rules the assistant applies to its work: brand guidelines, editorial policy, compliance rules, what can and can't be promised, style and voice. It is consulted while producing or reviewing work.

## What makes it usable
- Scope: which work the rules apply to (all public copy, only emails, only replies to clients).
- Each rule as an observable instruction, with its condition, its exception and, when it isn't obvious, its reason.
- Priority: what wins when two rules collide, or when a client's request contradicts a rule.
- For voice: the audience, the voice translated into concrete choices, preferred and avoided words, poor and good pairs.

## Diagnosis questions
- Which work will be checked against these rules?
- Which rules are mandatory, which are preferences, and which are habits nobody needs? Only the first two go in.
- What goes wrong most often today? Show a real case.
- Are there exceptions by channel, client, product or situation, and who decides when rules collide?
- Materials that help: the brand or style manual, the policy, approved texts, rejected texts with the reason, the corrections reviewers make most often.

## Typical skeleton
1. Scope, and what wins when rules collide
2. Rules grouped by the work they apply to
3. For voice: who we write for, the voice in concrete choices, words to use and avoid
4. Poor and good pairs for the rules that are hard to apply

## Rules for this type
- Group rules by the work they apply to (subject lines, body copy, offers, replies to complaints), so one section carries all the rules for that moment.
- Label each rule as mandatory or preferred when the material mixes both.
- Turn values and adjectives ("be authentic", "premium tone") into rules, and give each prohibition the alternative to use.
- Show a poor and good pair for each rule a description doesn't make clear (tone, rhythm, humor), and never let an example contradict a rule.
- When the manual and the approved texts disagree, ask which one is true instead of keeping both.

## Sample entry for the plan
One rule as it will appear, with its condition, exception and reason, and a poor and good pair if it needs one.
