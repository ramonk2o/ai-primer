# Type: checklist

Criteria the assistant checks work against before delivering or approving it: a publishing checklist, a contract review, a campaign QA. It is consulted at the end of a task, or when reviewing someone else's work.

## What makes it usable
- When the checklist is used, on what work, and what the review returns (a corrected version, a list of issues, a pass or fail).
- Criteria checked by looking at the work, each with what passes and what fails.
- Which criteria block delivery and which are recommendations.
- What to do when a criterion fails: fix it, flag it, or stop and escalate.

## Diagnosis questions
- On which work, and at which moment, is the check run?
- What do reviewers send back most often today?
- Which failures block delivery, and which only get a note?
- Do criteria depend on the channel, client or type of piece?
- Materials that help: an existing checklist, review comments on real work, a rejected piece with the reasons, the guidelines the checklist enforces.

## Typical skeleton
1. When to use it, and what the review returns
2. Blocking criteria
3. Recommended criteria
4. Criteria by channel or type, when they differ

## Rules for this type
- Each criterion is a yes or no question answered by looking at the work: "Does every price show the currency and two decimals?", not "Are prices correct?".
- Put the action next to the criterion when it isn't obvious ("If not, rewrite with the currency symbol").
- Order criteria as the review goes, or by how often they fail.
- Mark which criteria block delivery; a list of equal criteria makes the model treat a typo like a legal error.
- If a criterion checks a rule detailed in another file, name that file and section instead of restating the rule.
- Keep the procedure for producing the work out; criteria nobody fails are noise.

## Sample entry for the plan
Two criteria as they will appear, one blocking and one recommended, each with its action on failure.
